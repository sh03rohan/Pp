### Slide 2: Physics + CSE: Why They Connect

First, what is Physics? Physics is the study of energy, force, light, and matter. It explains how nature works. CSE is the study of computers, software, and hardware. Physics gives the rules, and CSE uses these rules to build technology. Now, look at this computer. First, electricity gives power. Then, the hardware, like the processor and memory, uses that power. Next, signals carry information. Finally, we get the result on the screen. For example, when you send a message on your phone, Physics carries it. Without Physics, there is no electricity, no chips, and no internet. Now, Shammy Sultana will explain electricity and electronics.

## Shammy Sultana — Electricity & Electronics

Slides 3–4 · 197 words · about 1:53

### Slide 3: Electricity: The Power Behind Computers

Thank you, Habibullah. Hello, everyone. I am Shammy Sultana. Electricity is the basic energy source of every computer. Computers need electrical power to do every task, like opening an app or playing a video. There are three important words. Voltage pushes electricity through a circuit. Current is the flow of electric charge. Resistance controls the flow of electricity. Look at this circuit. When I increase the voltage, more current flows. When I increase the resistance, less current flows. And when there is no voltage, nothing works. Without electricity, computers cannot function.

*Action: Move the Voltage slider up, then move the Resistance slider up. At the end, move Voltage to 0 to show the computer turn OFF.*

### Slide 4: Electronics: How Computers Understand Electricity

Now, how do computers understand electricity? The answer is electronics. Electronic components control the flow of electricity. The most important one is the transistor. A transistor acts like a tiny electronic switch. When it is ON, we call it one. When it is OFF, we call it zero. Computers use these ON and OFF signals to process information. For example, these eight switches make the letter A. A microchip contains millions or even billions of tiny transistors. These electrical circuits help the CPU perform calculations. Physics makes faster, smaller, and smarter computers possible. Now, Hridoy Dhar will show you how Physics helps us in real life.

*Action: Click the big switch to show ON and OFF. Then point to the eight switches that make the letter A.*

## Hridoy Dhar — Real-World Applications

Slides 5–6 · 189 words · about 1:48

### Slide 5: Light and Waves Connect Us

Thank you, Shammy. Hello, everyone. I am Hridoy Dhar. First, fiber optic cables. A fiber cable is a very thin thread of glass. It sends data as flashes of light. A flash means one, and no flash means zero. The light bounces inside the glass, so it stays inside the cable. Light is very fast, so we get fast internet. Most internet data between countries travels through these cables. Next, wireless communication. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and mobile networks use electromagnetic waves. These are invisible waves of energy, and they travel at the speed of light. Bluetooth connects close devices, like earphones. So, we can use the internet without any wires.

*Action: Click 'Send' to send the word through the cable. Then point to the Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and mobile cards.*

### Slide 6: Sensors and Robotics

Now, sensors and robots. A sensor helps a machine feel the world. It can detect movement, distance, light, and temperature. Look at this robot. It sends out a sound wave. The sound hits the box and comes back, like an echo. The robot measures the time and finds the distance. When the box is too close, the robot stops. Robot vacuum cleaners and self-driving cars use the same idea. Now, Sakibul Hasan Rohan will talk about the future of technology.

*Action: Watch the robot stop near the box. You can move the box with the slider.*

## Sakibul Hasan Rohan — Future & Conclusion

Slides 7–8 · 184 words · about 1:45

### Slide 7: Quantum Computing

Thank you, Hridoy. Hello, everyone. I am Sakibul Hasan Rohan. Let's look at the future. First, quantum computers. A normal computer uses bits. A bit is zero or one. A quantum computer uses qubits. A qubit can be in a mix of zero and one. When we measure it, we get zero or one. Quantum computers are not faster for everything. But they can help with special problems, like designing new medicines and materials. They are still new, and many need very cold temperatures. Big companies and universities are building them today.

*Action: Click 'Measure' a few times. Each time, the qubit gives 0 or 1.*

### Slide 8: The Future and Conclusion

Physics will also improve other technologies, like smart robots, Virtual Reality, Augmented Reality, and smaller, faster computer chips. These ideas start in Physics labs and become products for everyone. To conclude, Physics is the base of Computer Science and Engineering. Electricity powers our computers. Transistors and microchips help them think. Light and waves connect us. Sensors help machines understand the world. Physics explains how nature works, and CSE turns that knowledge into technology. Remember this: behind every great computer, there is great Physics. Thank you for listening. Do you have any questions?

*Action: Point to the four cards. Then slow down, say the last sentence clearly, and invite questions.*
